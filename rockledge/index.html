How Rockledge Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like discolored hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts today so you can reach us fast the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches Rockledge Homeowners Face

Most water heater failures we see in Rockledge come back to what's in the water. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, minerals can be part of the picture: dissolved minerals settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, insulating the water from the burner, and that's what causes the rumbling and popping many homeowners hear during a heating cycle. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, which is why regular descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

Age is the other big factor. Many homes in the area still have the original tank, and once a tank is past the ten-year mark, the anode rod has usually been consumed and corrosion starts working on the steel from the inside. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and elements, thermostats, and breakers on electric ones.

Code is another piece. Swapping out an older heater often means bringing the install up to current code: an expansion tank if the water system is closed, a relief valve drain line that terminates correctly, a flue that drafts correctly on gas units, and a drain pan or seismic straps where required. Well water brings its own challenges, such as heavier sediment, iron staining, and the rotten-egg smell that sometimes shows up when the water reacts with a standard anode rod. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much cheaper when caught early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one. Give us a call at (321) 542-0678 to set up a visit.
