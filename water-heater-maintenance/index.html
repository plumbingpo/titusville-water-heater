Here is what shortens most water heaters' lives: corrosion from the inside and mineral buildup on the bottom, both of which are largely preventable. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer sits between the flame and the water, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it builds up around the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, is slowly used up. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Titusville, FL, we see this pattern constantly, and most of it was preventable.

A Titusville Water Heater visit is designed to stop both of those processes early. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, remove and check the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we check element resistance and thermostat operation and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, verify the temperature setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

Tankless water heaters have their own maintenance needs. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers error codes, reduced flow, or temperature swings. We descale the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. When to Call for Water Heater Maintenance

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Small drips at the drain valve or connections
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap

The homeowner's side of maintenance is short: keep the area clear, watch for drips, and pay attention to new sounds. The rest carries more risk than it looks. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
