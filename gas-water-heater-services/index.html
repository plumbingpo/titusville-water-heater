Most gas water heaters in Titusville, FL work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve closes for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Titusville Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every Titusville call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

Once we know the cause, the repair is usually simple. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. When to Call for Gas Water Heater Services

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • Hot water that runs out faster than it used to
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • A flame that looks orange or floppy rather than steady and blue
  • A pilot that lights, then goes out when you release the knob
  • Signs of backdrafting, such as soot, condensation, or an exhaust smell at the vent
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility

Start with the hazards. Fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust laced with carbon monoxide come with every gas water heater. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Install carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home and test them monthly
  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Drain sediment from the bottom of the tank once or twice a year
