A wet floor beside the water heater is a call we hear about constantly, and the first question is always the same: where is it actually coming from? Water runs downhill and pools at the lowest point, so the puddle is often nowhere near the source. It might be the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge line, the drain valve near the bottom of the tank, the hot and cold connections on top, condensation on a cold tank, or a nearby pipe that has nothing to do with the heater. It might also be the tank itself, and that answer changes everything. In Titusville, FL, we trace the source before we touch a wrench.

Our diagnosis is methodical. We dry the area, check the relief valve outlet and discharge pipe, inspect the drain valve, and follow the hot and cold lines up to the fittings on top of the tank. On gas units, we check the combustion chamber for water, which can point to condensation or, more seriously, a failing tank. If the relief valve has been discharging, we put a gauge on the system to read static pressure and check for thermal expansion, since a closed plumbing system without an expansion tank can spike pressure every time the burner runs. Only then do we recommend a fix.

With the source confirmed, the fix is often simple. A new T&P valve, a brass drain valve in place of a weeping plastic one, fresh dielectric fittings or supply connectors, and an expansion tank or pressure-reducing valve when pressure is the cause can all be handled on the spot. A corroded tank is different. Nothing will reliably seal a rusted tank, so we explain replacement honestly and walk through the choices. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A water bill that rises without any change in usage
  • Steady drips from the drain valve near the base
  • Water dripping from the relief valve or out of the end of its discharge pipe
  • Moisture or rust flakes under the burner access cover
  • Warped baseboards or stained drywall beside the water heater
  • Brown hot water along with moisture around the tank bottom
  • A wet floor near the water heater that keeps coming back
  • Corroded pipe connections or damp flex lines above the heater

Don't Wait for a Total Failure

Most water heater failures start small: a popping sound from sediment, a damp spot under the drain valve, or a shower that goes lukewarm a few minutes sooner each week. If you notice any of these signs, contact Titusville Water Heater at (321) 542-0678 for a prompt inspection.

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Leak Repair

Water heater leaks are easy to misread. Water pooling at the base may start at a fitting on top and run down the jacket, and condensation on a cold tank can look exactly like a failure. Buying the wrong part is money down the drain, and overlooking a tank that is starting to go can lead to a much bigger flood later. There are safety concerns, too. The relief valve guards the tank against runaway heat and pressure, and blocking it to stop a drip can set up a violent failure. Tightening corroded fittings on an old tank can crack them, and electric heaters sit on 240-volt circuits that do not mix well with wet floors.

Our technicians trace leaks methodically and fix the cause, not just the drip. We dry the unit, let new water reveal the source, put a gauge on the system whenever the relief valve is involved, and check for thermal expansion problems that make valves weep. We replace valves and fittings with durable replacements, swap plastic drain valves for brass, and add an expansion tank or pressure-reducing valve when pressure is the root problem. When the tank is the problem, we say so directly and explain replacement options, including the drain pan and leak protection that can keep the next failure from turning into water damage.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • A battery leak alarm beside the heater costs little and can catch a problem days sooner
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years; once it is used up, the tank starts to corrode
  • Flush the tank once a year so sediment does not overheat the bottom and speed corrosion
  • Glance at the heater monthly for damp spots, rust, or crust on the fittings
  • Ask about expansion control if your home has a check valve or backflow preventer
  • High street pressure is hard on water heaters, so a pressure test and regulator can prevent repeat drips
  • Place the heater in a drain pan with a line to a floor drain or outdoors, especially above finished space
