Upfront Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Before we touch a tool, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check the age and size of the tank, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any rust or seepage around the fittings. After that you get an honest recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is swapping a thermocouple or installing a new heater. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment settles on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod quietly corrodes away, and the tank lining starts to rust from the inside with no outward warning. If you notice rusty hot water, popping or rumbling sounds, a rotten-egg smell, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, or moisture around the base, schedule an inspection sooner rather than later. An early diagnosis usually keeps a fixable issue from turning into a flooded floor.

How old is your water heater? The date code on the label usually gives it away. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one decides for you. For reference, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Annual Flushing

Maintenance Plans for Titusville Homes

A maintenance visit from Titusville Water Heater is far more than opening the drain valve. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, lifts and tests the relief valve, checks the burner, pilot, and venting on gas heaters, tests the elements and thermostats on electric units, and confirms a safe water temperature. Minor faults are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and the heater stays running clean and efficient.

Most homes do best with one flush a year, and homes with hard water may need a visit every six months. We adjust the schedule to the age of your heater, how hard your household works it, and the mineral content of your water, which determines how quickly sediment accumulates and how long an anode rod lasts.

The payoff shows up quickly. A tank without a sediment crust transfers heat with less effort, a replaced anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Next to the cost of replacing a tank early, a maintenance plan is a bargain.

Proper Sizing

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

An undersized water heater runs out before everyone has showered, while an oversized one burns fuel keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Titusville Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your morning rush, not just copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute against the fixtures you run at once and the temperature of your incoming water.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water needs. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely keeps up once kids are showering every morning, and a tankless water heater can deliver steady hot water so long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: upfront cost, operating cost, venting and gas line sizing, electrical service, and the footprint, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

No hot water, a burner that refuses to light, a tank full of sediment, a slow drip from the relief valve: each of these problems has a different cause and needs its own repair. Titusville Water Heater tracks down the exact fault before swapping any parts, whether it's an electric tank with a burned-out element, a gas heater with a failing thermocouple, a broken dip tube mixing cold water into the hot, or a tankless unit throwing error codes.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A bad element just means lukewarm showers. A relief valve that discharges, on the other hand, signals that pressure or temperature inside the tank is too high, and water pooling under the tank can soak into flooring before anyone notices. If you smell gas, get everyone outside and call the gas utility first. Our technicians carry the parts to replace elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, install expansion tanks where pressure is the problem, and handle leak repair at the drain valve or fittings.
