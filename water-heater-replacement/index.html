Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • Discolored hot water, a sign the tank is corroding inside
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area

Small Symptoms, Big Consequences

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. A quick call to (321) 542-0678 gets a technician out to check it before it becomes an emergency.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A water heater replacement touches more systems than most people expect. Gas work covers the supply line, sediment trap, shutoff, and a vent that must draft well enough to keep exhaust out of the house. Electrical work means matching breaker and wire gauge to the element wattage. The tank needs a relief valve of the correct rating piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models pile on more: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

A professional replacement covers the whole installation, not just the tank. Sizing is done by first-hour rating or flow rate. Fuel, venting, and electrical capacity are verified, and whatever expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs get added. We leak-test every joint, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and haul the old heater off. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
